If you’re a sole trader or landlord whose qualifying income was over £50,000 in 2024 to 2025, Making Tax Digital for Income Tax has applied to you since 6 April 2026. The threshold drops to £30,000 from April 2027 and £20,000 from April 2028, according to gov.uk. You’ll need software HMRC recognises. Zoho Books has a free plan, FreeAgent is free while you keep a NatWest, RBS, Ulster Bank or Mettle business account, and Xero starts at £7 a month for businesses not registered for VAT.

  • Qualifying income

    Your total income from self-employment and property, before expenses. Wages from a job, a partnership profit share, dividends and pensions don’t count.

  • Four updates a year

    You send HMRC a quarterly update by 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May, then a final return.

  • No penalty yet for late updates

    Gov.uk says there are no penalties for missing a quarterly update deadline in the 2026 to 2027 tax year. Late tax returns still earn penalty points.

This is a summary, not tax advice. Check your own position on gov.uk or with an accountant. Some people can apply for an exemption.
