UAE and Oman guide for every small business

VAT, corporate tax and e-invoicing

By Prejith Ponnachan · Updated 17 November 2026

1 July 2027

E-invoicing becomes mandatory for UAE businesses with revenue under AED 50 million, on invoices to other businesses and government. Sales to consumers are outside it.

  • Choose an accredited provider by 31 March 2027.
  • VAT is 5%. You must register once sales pass AED 375,000 in 12 months.
  • Small Business Relief runs to tax periods ending 31 December 2029, for revenue of AED 3 million or less.

In the UAE, VAT is 5% and you must register once taxable sales pass AED 375,000 in 12 months. Corporate tax is 0% on profit up to AED 375,000 and 9% above it, and businesses with revenue of AED 3 million or less can claim Small Business Relief for tax periods ending by 31 December 2029. E-invoicing becomes mandatory for businesses under AED 50 million revenue from 1 July 2027, on invoices to other businesses and government. In Oman, VAT is 5%, with registration from OMR 38,500.

The UAE rules

RuleWhat it says
VAT5%. Register when taxable sales and imports pass AED 375,000 in the last 12 months, or will in the next 30 days. You can register voluntarily from AED 187,500
Corporate tax0% on taxable income up to AED 375,000. 9% above it
Small Business ReliefRevenue of AED 3 million or less in this and every earlier tax period, for tax periods ending on or before 31 December 2029. Not for qualifying free zone businesses. You still file a return to claim it
E-invoicing, revenue of AED 50m or moreAppoint an accredited provider by 30 October 2026. Mandatory from 1 January 2027
E-invoicing, revenue under AED 50mAppoint an accredited provider by 31 March 2027. Mandatory from 1 July 2027

E-invoicing covers invoices to other businesses and to government. Sales to consumers are outside it. Most small businesses fall in the second group, so the date to plan for is 1 July 2027, with a provider chosen by 31 March 2027.
